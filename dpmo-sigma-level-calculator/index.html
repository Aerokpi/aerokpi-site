02Worked example

Circuit board inspection. 500 boards, 10 checked features per board, 31 defects found.

  • DPU = 31 / 500 = 0.062
  • DPO = 31 / 5,000 = 0.0062
  • DPMO = 6,200
  • Sigma = NORM.S.INV(0.9938) + 1.5 = 2.50 + 1.5 = 4.00

For comparison, the standard table gives 6,210 DPMO at exactly 4 sigma, so this line is right at 4 sigma.

03How it works

DPU = defects / units DPO = defects / (units × opportunities per unit) DPMO = DPO × 1,000,000 Sigma = NORM.S.INV(1 - DPO) + 1.5 (short-term, with shift) Sigma = NORM.S.INV(1 - DPO) (long-term, no shift)

An opportunity is one place a defect could occur and that you actually check: a dimension, a solder joint, a field on a form. Define opportunities before you count, and keep the definition fixed. Adding opportunities that never fail makes the sigma level look better without improving anything.

The 1.5 sigma shift is a convention that assumes a process drifts by about 1.5 standard deviations over the long term. With the shift, 3.4 DPMO is 6 sigma. Without it, 3.4 DPMO is about 4.5 sigma. State which one you use when you report a sigma level.

First-pass yield estimate uses e−DPU, the Poisson approximation of the share of units with zero defects.

04FAQ

What is the difference between DPU and DPMO?

DPU counts defects per unit, whatever the unit's complexity. DPMO divides by the number of opportunities, so simple and complex products can be compared on the same scale.

Why is 6 sigma 3.4 DPMO?

That figure includes the 1.5 sigma shift. A process at 6 sigma short-term is assumed to perform at 4.5 sigma long-term, and the normal tail beyond 4.5 sigma is 3.4 per million.

Should I use the 1.5 shift?

Use it if you are comparing with standard Six Sigma tables, and say so in the report. If you measured over a long period that already includes drift, many practitioners report long-term sigma without the shift.

What if I found zero defects?

A sigma level can't be calculated from zero defects. It only means the true rate is low compared with your sample size. Inspect more units before claiming a level.

How do I calculate this in Excel?

DPMO = defects / (units * opportunities) * 1000000. Sigma = NORM.S.INV(1 - DPMO/1000000) + 1.5.
