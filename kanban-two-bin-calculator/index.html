02Worked example

Fastener at an assembly cell. 120 used per day, 3-day replenishment loop, 20% safety factor, packs of 50.

  • Lead-time demand = 120 × 3 = 360
  • Safety stock = 360 × 20% = 72
  • Required = 432, so 432 / 50 = 8.64, rounded up to 9 kanban cards
  • Two-bin: 450 per bin (9 packs), 900 maximum on hand

Using the variation method instead (σ = 30 per day, 95% service level) gives safety stock of about 85.5 and a required quantity of about 446.

03How it works

A kanban loop has to hold enough stock to cover usage while a replenishment order is on its way, plus a buffer for variation. The same logic sizes each bin in a two-bin system: when the first bin empties, the second must last until the first comes back full.

Lead-time demand = daily usage × lead time (days) Safety stock = lead-time demand × safety % (percent method) = z × σdaily × √lead time (variation method) Required qty = lead-time demand + safety stock Kanban cards = roundup(required qty / container size) Qty per bin = roundup(required qty / pack size) × pack size

Lead time means the whole replenishment loop: the time for the empty bin or card to reach the supplier, plus the time to make or pick the parts, plus the time to deliver them back to the point of use. Leaving out the signal time is the most common reason two-bin systems run dry.

Recalculate when demand or lead time changes by more than about 20%, and at least once a quarter.

04FAQ

What is the kanban formula?

Number of kanbans = (daily demand × lead time × (1 + safety factor)) / container quantity, always rounded up. This is the version most Lean references use.

How do I size a two-bin system?

Each bin must hold enough to cover usage during the full replenishment lead time plus safety stock. Round up to whole packs so the bins are easy to refill.

What safety factor should I use?

10% to 30% is typical when usage is fairly steady. If usage swings a lot from day to day, use the variation method with the standard deviation of daily usage and a service level.

Why always round up?

Rounding down leaves the loop short. You would run out before the replenishment arrives every time demand hits the average.

What does service level mean?

It's the chance of not running out during one replenishment cycle. 95% corresponds to z = 1.645. Higher service levels need more safety stock.

Does this work for supermarkets and line-side racks too?

Yes. Any pull system that reorders on a signal can be sized the same way. Use the time from signal to refill as the lead time.
